Does everything really collide as grains?

You asked me to think this rather than take it as read, and the honest answer is no — and that is a large saving. Three tiers, and only the middle one needs grain-by-grain collision:

WHOLE — rock, metal, wood, ice, bone, a star. These never collide as grains because they are never grains: they are one record with a wear counter, and grains only exist once liberated. Most of the world, by volume, is this tier. cost: one object
GRANULAR — sand, gravel, ash, soot, powder, snow, salt, gunpowder. These are the only materials that need real grain collision: they pile, they hold an angle of repose, they pour through a gap. cost: the falling-sand grid, and only where grains exist
FLUID & GAS — water, oil, lava, acid, blood, steam, smoke, plasma. These never collide as grains either. They move by density and pressure, which is cheaper than contact: who sinks through whom is one comparison. cost: a flow field, no contacts at all

So the expensive answer — every particle testing every neighbour — is needed for perhaps a tenth of what is on screen. The rest is either a whole object or a fluid, and neither has grains to collide. That is why matter.js keeps objects intact and only spawns particles where matter has actually come off.
